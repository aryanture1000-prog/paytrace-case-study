# Go-to-market plan, October 2026

**Why:** cold LinkedIn connections were not converting, so I replaced them with free channels that fit who PayTrace sells to and what the product can prove.

## Core idea

The best free asset is the product's own output. A brand uploads its courier files and sees how many rupees were short-paid. Lead with a free audit, not a pitch, and turn each result into proof.

## Channels, ranked

1. **Free audits for 10 brands,** done by me. Goal: three real case studies, with permission.
2. **Answer problems where operators ask them,** such as weight discrepancies and COD remittances not received, on Reddit, X, Shopify Community and Facebook groups. Help first, and mention PayTrace only if asked or when it is the actual fix.
3. **Free tools:** a volumetric weight calculator, a COD leakage calculator and a weight-dispute letter generator.
4. **Partners:** D2C and Shopify agencies, and accountants who do books for D2C brands. The Pro tier fits agencies, so I would offer 30 days of free Pro in return for feedback.
5. **LinkedIn, content-first:** anonymised real audit findings with permission, no cold messages.
6. **Directories** for backlinks, which convert slowly.
7. **Referral:** one free month per paying referral.

## Fix before sending traffic

Confirm the payment-flow bugs from my own audit are closed in the live build, add a consent line for terms and privacy at signup (people upload courier data), and add a sitemap.

## 30 days

- Week 1: ship the fixes and the first calculator, build a list of 40 brands that complain in public, and offer 10 free audits.
- Week 2: run the audits, record before and after rupee figures, ask for permission to publish, and start a weekly listening routine.
- Week 3: publish the first case study and the COD leakage calculator, and approach 10 agencies and 5 accountants.
- Week 4: courier-specific dispute pages and LinkedIn posts from real findings, then review the numbers.

## What I will measure

Audits offered, audits accepted, trials started, trial to paid, rupees found per audit, and agencies onboarded. **Stop rule:** if fewer than 3 of 10 audit offers are accepted, the problem is the offer or the targeting, not the channel.

## Limits of the evidence

Some market figures I found come from vendors and are unsourced, so I do not quote them as fact. I had no LinkedIn accept or reply rates, so why the cold approach failed is unconfirmed. I have not verified the specific groups where operators gather, and I will find them by hand.
